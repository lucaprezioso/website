Driving a Ferrari for the first time. What to expect from your rental in Zurich

The first time you sit behind a Ferrari steering wheel should feel exciting, with enough time to feel comfortable. This practical guide takes you from your first enquiry to the return of our Ferrari 458 Italia near Zurich.

The essentials before your first drive

You do not need to have owned or driven a Ferrari before to enquire. You do need to meet the rental requirements and feel comfortable driving an unfamiliar, powerful car. At Luxury Obsession, a personal introduction and a short accompanied familiarisation drive help you get to know the 458 before setting off on your own. Choose a manageable route, leave time for stops and ask every question you need to.

Is a Ferrari 458 difficult to drive?

The unfamiliarity can be more intimidating than the actual task of driving. You sit lower, see a different shape in the mirrors and hear the engine much more clearly than in an ordinary car. The controls also deserve a proper introduction. None of that means your first drive needs to be stressful.

The 458 has a dual-clutch transmission with an automatic mode, so you do not have to learn a manual clutch. At the same time, its direct responses and substantial performance call for measured inputs and attention. Think of the first kilometres as familiarisation, not an opportunity to test what the car can do. The best first experience is one where your confidence grows gradually.

Who can rent the Ferrari for a first experience?

A first Ferrari drive is different from being a newly qualified driver. Previous supercar experience is something we can discuss, while age, driving licence and booking eligibility must be checked before confirmation. Our published minimum age is 21. Tell us your age, how long you have held your licence and whether you are visiting from abroad, so we can confirm the documentation required for your situation.

Make sure every intended driver is approved in writing. Bringing someone as a passenger does not authorise them to take the wheel later. Start with our Ferrari rental in Zurich page and check the current packages and conditions. If you are uncertain about your eligibility, ask before arranging a surprise or committing to travel.

What happens at the handover?

We agree the pickup location and booking details beforehand. At handover, we check the required documents and walk around the Ferrari together. This is the moment to look at the wheels, bodywork and interior, understand the condition record and confirm the fuel and return arrangements.

Inside, we introduce the driving position, mirrors, essential controls, transmission and the driving mode appropriate for the conditions. We also explain practical details such as refuelling and parking. You should know how to operate the indicators, lights and wipers before you need them in traffic.

A short accompanied familiarisation drive, usually around 10 to 15 minutes, lets you ask questions while experiencing the car. This introduction takes place before your booked driving time begins. Confirm the departure and return times together so you can enjoy the full agreed rental period without guessing.

Getting comfortable in the cockpit

Take your time getting in and setting up. Adjust the seat so you can operate the pedals comfortably and hold the steering wheel without stretching. Set both mirrors before moving, fasten your belt and make sure your passenger is settled too. Comfortable, secure footwear makes more sense than shoes chosen only for photographs.

Our Ferrari’s black interior and carbon details are part of the occasion, but the useful first step is learning where everything is. Ask us to show you the displays and controls you will use during your journey. Set navigation and audio while parked, secure loose items and keep the driver’s footwell clear. A few calm minutes here save distractions later.

Automatic mode, paddles and the manettino

You can let the transmission change gear automatically or use the paddles after we have explained them. There is no requirement to spend the day manually shifting simply because you are driving a Ferrari. Automatic mode can make the first stretch easier to manage while you get used to the car’s dimensions and response.

The manettino is the driving-mode selector on the steering wheel. Its settings affect how the car behaves; they are not a checklist to try during a rental. Follow the handover guidance and keep the stability and traction systems active. Do not choose a setting just because its name sounds more exciting.

We demonstrate selecting drive, reversing and securing the parked car in person. This guide complements that introduction and is not a substitute for the car’s operating instructions. If something is unclear, ask while stationary rather than experimenting in traffic.

Your first kilometres: a calm start

Choose a straightforward first stretch with good visibility and room to settle in. You are learning the steering response, pedal feel and where the body sits in the lane. Smooth acceleration, gentle steering inputs and plenty of space from other vehicles are more useful than trying to make the drive look impressive.

Allow the car to warm up as explained during the handover. Build familiarity before tackling dense city traffic or a narrow mountain road. Avoid treating an empty stretch as permission for sudden acceleration, and keep your attention on signs and other road users rather than the sound of the engine.

It is perfectly reasonable to stop at a suitable parking place, reset your navigation and take a breath. A successful first Ferrari drive is not measured by how quickly you stop feeling cautious.

Why it feels special without driving fast

The Ferrari 458 Italia combines a naturally aspirated V8 behind the cabin with a low seating position and a very immediate connection to the road. The pleasure comes from the combination: the view over the front wings, the sound, a smooth change of gear and a well-chosen stretch of countryside.

You do not need to chase high revs or speed to appreciate it. A lake road and an unhurried afternoon can be just as memorable as a mountain pass. For more on the car’s character, read our Ferrari 458 vs 488 comparison. The aim of a first rental is to enjoy the car in a setting that suits you, not to recreate a dramatic online clip.

Parking, reversing and low ground clearance

Slow manoeuvres deserve just as much attention as driving. The 458 sits low, and kerbs, steep ramps or abrupt changes in gradient can be awkward. Check an unfamiliar hotel or car park entrance before entering. If you cannot judge the clearance, stop and ask for guidance rather than assuming the car will fit.

Pick a legal, paved space with enough room to open the long doors. Leave a sensible margin around the wheels and neighbouring vehicles. When reversing, move slowly and check the surroundings directly; do not assume the car has the same cameras or parking aids as your everyday vehicle.

A passenger can help observe from a safe position if needed, but the driver remains responsible. Choosing an easier parking space a short walk away is often the most relaxing decision of the day.

A passenger, luggage and everyday comfort

The 458 Italia has two seats, so one passenger can share the experience. Plan the day for both of you: agree on breaks, choose a route you both enjoy and let your passenger handle navigation where helpful. Photographs are easiest when the car is safely parked, without creating pressure on the driver.

Luggage space is limited compared with a normal holiday car. Compact soft bags offer more flexibility than large rigid cases, but check your actual luggage with us before booking a longer trip. If you are tall, have mobility concerns or need a particular seating arrangement, raise it in advance so we can discuss suitability.

For child passengers, contact us before booking to confirm suitability and the applicable restraint requirements. Do not assume a two-seater will suit every family outing. Keep the cabin tidy and follow the rental rules on smoking, food and drinks.

Which route is best for a first Ferrari drive?

Start with the duration and your comfort level, then choose the road. Around Nänikon and Zurich, a countryside or lake itinerary can provide variety without making a high Alpine pass the first challenge. Traffic, roadworks and weather matter more than whether a route looks dramatic in a photograph.

Our Pfannenstiel and Meilen client experience shows the appeal of a shorter outing. The scenic drives from Zurich guide offers more ideas. Use them as inspiration, then allow time for breaks and the drive back.

For a mountain trip, discuss your route with us and check current pass openings and weather. The Andermatt and Alpine passes article is a longer-trip reference, not an instruction to fit several passes into your first few hours. A simple route driven comfortably is a better introduction than an ambitious schedule.

Three hours, six hours, a day or longer?

Three hours can suit a first taste of the Ferrari with a compact local route and a photo stop. Keep restaurant plans and long detours modest. The point is to enjoy the available time rather than spend it watching the return deadline.

Six hours creates more room for a varied route and a relaxed break. It can be a good balance when you want the drive to feel like a day out rather than a brief introduction.

A full day or 24 hours allows a wider plan, with the exact driving window depending on the package. An overnight rental also means arranging suitable parking. Several days make sense when the car is part of a holiday, as in our Ferrari journey to Maranello.

Compare duration and included kilometres on the pricing page. More time does not automatically mean more included distance, and unlimited kilometres never make a rushed itinerary a good idea. Tell us what you want to do and we can discuss a suitable package.

What if it rains or the weather changes?

Do not plan the whole experience around perfect weather. Reduced grip, standing water, cold conditions and poor visibility change what is appropriate. Use the settings explained at handover, keep assistance systems active, reduce your pace and leave more space. No electronic system removes the need to adapt to the conditions.

If the planned route no longer looks suitable, choose a simpler alternative or stop somewhere safe and contact us. For an Alpine trip, a lower route can be a sensible option. Discuss weather-related booking questions before confirmation; do not assume a particular cancellation or rescheduling arrangement applies.

Deposit, insurance and costs to understand beforehand

A clear booking makes the drive more relaxing. Know the rental duration, included mileage, excess-kilometre charge, fuel arrangement, pickup costs if applicable and the agreed return time. Our published deposit is CHF 1,500 for drivers aged 25 and over and CHF 3,000 for drivers under 25. The terms confirmed for your booking govern the arrangement.

The deposit and insurance excess are different concepts. Do not assume that paying a deposit means all possible liability is capped at that amount. Read the insurance conditions, excess and exclusions in your contract, and ask us to explain anything unclear before signing.

Driving abroad requires prior written approval. Track use, racing and drifting are not part of the rental experience. This is a road-going Ferrari journey with clear conditions, not an opportunity to explore the car’s limits. Current package information is kept on the rates page.

Fuel, unexpected issues and returning the Ferrari

We explain the fuel requirement at handover: use Super 98 as instructed and return the car full. Plan a suitable fuel stop near the end of the route, keep the receipt and leave time for the return inspection. Refuelling costs and any applicable handling charge follow the agreed contract if the car is not returned as required.

If a warning message appears, the car behaves unexpectedly or there is an incident, stop safely when appropriate and contact us. Follow any urgent dashboard instruction. Do not attempt repairs or continue merely to meet a timetable. Keep the contact details provided with your booking accessible.

At return, we check the vehicle together and review fuel, mileage and condition. Contact us as soon as a delay becomes likely; do not try to recover time by rushing. Remove your belongings and allow enough time for a proper handback.

Is a first Ferrari drive a good gift?

It can be a very personal gift when the recipient genuinely wants to drive a Ferrari. Check the driver requirements first, consider their confidence and choose a duration that allows them to enjoy the experience at their own pace. A surprise should not become pressure to drive an unfamiliar car.

Our Ferrari gift vouchers explain the available format and arrangements. The birthday drive to Kyburg shows how a first supercar experience can become a shared occasion. A thoughtful plan, a suitable route and someone to share it with often matter more than making the day elaborate.

Your checklist before setting off

  • Confirm driver approval, documents, deposit and the booking conditions.
  • Choose comfortable shoes and check luggage and passenger requirements.
  • Allow time for the introduction and accompanied familiarisation drive.
  • Set the seat, mirrors and navigation before moving.
  • Confirm the agreed driving mode, fuel requirement and return time.
  • Plan a manageable route, suitable parking and time for refuelling.
  • Keep our contact details handy and ask whenever something is unclear.

Your first Ferrari drive does not need a perfect itinerary. It needs a comfortable start and enough time to enjoy the car. Send us your preferred date, how long you would like to drive and whether this is your first supercar experience. We can then discuss the right starting point near Zurich.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need previous Ferrari experience?

You can enquire without previous Ferrari experience. Driver eligibility must be confirmed, and the handover includes an introduction and a short accompanied familiarisation drive.

Can I drive the Ferrari 458 in automatic mode?

Yes. The dual-clutch transmission has an automatic mode. We also explain the paddles at handover, so you can choose an appropriate approach without feeling obliged to shift manually.

Does the introduction use up my booked driving time?

The introduction and short accompanied familiarisation drive take place before your booked driving time begins. Departure and return times are confirmed together at handover.

Can I bring a passenger?

The Ferrari 458 Italia has two seats, with space for the driver and one passenger. A passenger must be separately approved as a driver before taking the wheel.

Is three hours enough for a first Ferrari drive?

Three hours can suit a compact local route and a short stop. Six hours gives more space for breaks and a varied outing. Choose based on your route and comfort, not only the rental price.

What deposit should I expect?

The published deposit is CHF 1,500 for drivers aged 25 and over and CHF 3,000 for drivers under 25. Confirm the terms for your booking and read the insurance excess separately.

Can I take the Ferrari to Italy?

Travel to Italy requires prior written approval and the applicable agreed supplement. Discuss the full journey and mileage before confirming the rental.

What should I do if I feel unsure during the drive?

Find a safe, suitable place to stop and contact us. Do not adjust unfamiliar controls or try to resolve a problem while driving.
