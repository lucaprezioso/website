Car photo spots near Zurich and Uster: the Ferrari in the frame

Historic façades, elegant hotel arrivals and quiet green backgrounds: ideas for photographs with the Ferrari, illustrated with our own 458 Italia.

Published 21 Feb 2026 · Updated 25 Oct 2026

A beautiful destination becomes a good car-photo location when the background complements the car and there is a suitable place to position it. Around Zurich and Uster, we would start with the village around Schloss Kyburg for historic character, the Dolder Grand for an elegant hotel setting, and Museum Bellerive as an architectural idea to plan in advance.

The Ferrari stays at the centre of this guide. Our photographs at Kyburg and the Dolder Grand show how a low sports car works against very different surroundings. You do not have to fit an entire landmark into the frame: a wall, a doorway or a clean line of trees can be enough.

Schloss Kyburg: village character with the Ferrari

Our photographs from a Ferrari outing to Kyburg show the car against the village’s historic architecture. The old façades, shutters and textured walls create a setting that feels distinctly Swiss, while the red bodywork gives the image its focal point.

The photograph to try
Try a rear three-quarter photograph with a building framing the car, then move to a clean side profile. Keep enough space around the roof and bumpers, and watch for signs or branches that appear to grow out of the car. In the side view, the 458’s low roofline and sculpted door are the main attraction.
Access and timing
The castle’s official arrival page directs drivers to parking before the village. Choose the exact stopping position according to current signs and any necessary permission; our earlier photographs do not guarantee that the same space will be available on another day. Soft light or open shade helps avoid bright patches on the bodywork.

Museum Bellerive: an architectural idea in Zurich’s Seefeld

Museum Bellerive, now home to ZAZ Bellerive at Höschgasse 3, is a worthwhile addition for a more urban photograph. Its pale façade offers the kind of restrained architectural background that can make Rosso Corsa stand out without filling the image with visual clutter.

The photograph to try
The idea is a front three-quarter view with a small section of the façade behind the car. Before deciding on the composition, check which side can actually be seen from a permitted stopping position. A tighter frame can be stronger than trying to include the whole villa and the lake.
Access and timing
Treat this as a location to scout and arrange, rather than an established Luxury Obsession shoot. Public parking nearby is limited. If your chosen position uses the forecourt or museum grounds, ask the venue about access first. A parking space nearby does not automatically offer the desired angle.

The Dolder Grand: the atmosphere of a special arrival

The Dolder Grand gives a Ferrari photograph a different mood: a grand façade, an arrival setting and a sense of occasion. Our existing photographs show how well the 458 works here, both as the main subject and with the architecture giving context.

The photograph to try
For a front view, keep the camera low enough to emphasise the nose but high enough to retain the building’s shape. A three-quarter view shows more of the sculpted side. For a photograph with your partner or a friend, leave space beside the car instead of placing the person directly in front of the bonnet.
Access and timing
The hotel lists paid parking, but arranging a photograph at the entrance is a separate matter. Agree the position and timing with the hotel team, keeping arrivals and the entrance clear. Soft late-day light can be attractive when it suits the agreed time; overcast conditions can also flatter the red paint.

Around Uster and Nänikon: let the car do the work

A recognisable landmark is optional. Around the starting area in Nänikon and Uster, look for a suitable place with a calm background: greenery, a simple façade or a clean stretch of wall. This is particularly useful when you want a few good photographs without making a separate trip into Zurich.

The photograph to try
A side profile works well against greenery because it shows the Ferrari’s proportions clearly. Keep the camera roughly level with the door, step back and avoid an extreme wide-angle setting. For a second image, move to the rear three-quarter angle rather than only taking more pictures from the same position.
Access and timing
Pick a lawful stopping place with enough space for the car and the photographer. We can discuss an appropriate setting when planning your rental; this is an approach to choosing a background, not a promise of access to a specific field, driveway or private property.

Five small choices that improve a car photograph

Make the car the focal point

Choose the background first, then check what appears around the roofline and wheels. Bins, signs and another parked car are often more distracting than you noticed in person.

Use light that shows the shape

Soft side light or open shade helps describe the curves of the 458. Harsh midday sun can create bright reflections and dark shadows. Move yourself a little before deciding the light is wrong.

Avoid stretching the car

On a phone, start with the main camera rather than the ultra-wide lens. Step back for a more natural shape and keep the phone level. Photograph the car while it is stationary.

Take three different views

Aim for one front three-quarter view, one side or rear view, and one personal photograph beside the car. A small varied set is usually more useful than twenty nearly identical frames.

Allow time for the photograph

Choose one or two settings and leave time to find the angle. If the background is busy or the stopping position is unsuitable, use another spot instead of forcing the picture.
