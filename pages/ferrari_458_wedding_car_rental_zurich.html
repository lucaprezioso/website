Planning a Ferrari Wedding: Timeline, Photos & Checklist

The arrival is only one part of a wedding with a Ferrari. There is also the first drive together, a quiet moment in the cockpit and the photographs you will return to long after the celebration. A little preparation gives those moments room to happen without rushing between appointments.

Published 25.02.2026 · Updated 29.10.2026

This guide helps you fit a self-drive Ferrari 458 Italia into a wedding in Zurich and the surrounding area. Start with the ceremony and reception times, then work backwards through the journeys, handover and photographs. Start with the moments you want to share

Decide what role the car should play: the arrival at a civil ceremony, a drive between two venues, couple portraits or a journey together after the reception. This gives you a useful starting point for the rental window. A car used mainly for photographs needs a different plan from one accompanying you throughout the day.

Write down the addresses and the times that cannot move. Then add the photographer’s availability, access to each venue and the agreed return. The Ferrari has two seats: the couple can travel together, but a photographer, other guests and larger bags need separate transport.

Choose the rental window around the whole day

Driving time is only part of the booking. The car may wait during the ceremony, family photographs and reception, so calculate the full period from the agreed rental start to return. A quiet interval in the middle of the day does not stop the rental clock.

For a short civil ceremony, one arrival and nearby photographs may be enough. With several venues, allow for each journey and parking stop. If an evening return would interrupt the celebration, discuss an overnight arrangement in advance. Compare the available durations on the wedding booking page rather than choosing solely by the number of kilometres.

The dress, the doors and the first few minutes

The 458 has a low seating position. Discuss a voluminous dress, train or long veil before the day, and allow time for an unhurried entry and exit. There is no single fit rule for every dress: the shape and fabric matter. Choose a level stopping place with enough room to open the passenger door fully.

A helper can hold the train clear of the ground while the passenger settles in. Before moving, check that fabric is clear of the door, the seat belt sits correctly and the driver’s footwear allows comfortable pedal control. Arrange separate transport for bulky bags and anything you will not need during the drive.

Check the venue access, not just the address

Ask the venue where the car can stop and remain while you are inside. A scenic entrance is not automatically a usable arrival point. Check steep ramps, abrupt changes of level, uneven gravel, narrow gates and space to turn without a rushed manoeuvre.

Give the venue and photographer one agreed contact person. Confirm who will open a gate, where guests will gather and whether the photo position requires permission. Keep the car’s access route clear and choose a simpler alternative if the intended entrance is unsuitable.

Plan the photographs as a small part of the story

Agree a short shot list with your photographer before the wedding. The two photographs below show complementary ideas: an exterior portrait that includes the dress and architecture, and a close, personal moment inside the car. Keep the couple at the centre of the image.

Five photographs to discuss with your photographer

  1. Arrival: agree a safe stopping point so the photographer can be ready without directing a moving car at close range.
  2. Exterior portrait: leave space for the dress and use the Ferrari’s shape as part of the composition.
  3. Cockpit portrait: photograph the couple while the car is parked and stationary.
  4. Details: a bouquet, hands or the steering wheel can complement the wider photographs; keep jewellery and props clear of paintwork.
  5. Departure: coordinate the photographer’s position before setting off.

Keep a few essential photographs and leave room for spontaneous moments. If you need a photographer, we can recommend professional contacts. Discuss availability, style and the photography service directly with them.

Prepare an evening plan and a weather alternative

Decide who will drive each part of the day and make sure every driver is approved in advance. If the driver will drink at the reception, arrange the onward journey and return differently before the wedding. A two-seat self-drive Ferrari is not a chauffeur car.

For rain, agree a sheltered stopping place and an alternative photo setting with the venue. Do not assume a covered entrance is available or accessible. Weather-related changes to the rental must be discussed with us; a photo backup plan is separate from a change to the booking.

Include a convenient fuel stop in the return plan: the car is collected with a full tank and returned full. Confirm the return location and time, and avoid leaving the celebration simply because this last part was overlooked.
