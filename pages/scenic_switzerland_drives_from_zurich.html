Scenic drives from Zurich

Three routes, from lakeside stops to a full Alpine day. Compare realistic time allowances, distances and rental options before you set off.

Updated 21 October 2026

A scenic drive from Zurich can mean a relaxed afternoon beside Lake Zurich or a full day on the Susten, Grimsel and Furka passes. The right choice depends on your available time, the weather and how much driving you actually want to do. A mountain loop that looks compact on a map can take most of the day once you include the journey from Zurich, lunch and photo stops.

We have organised this guide around three different days out: lakes and historic towns, Lake Brienz via the Brünig Pass, and a demanding high-Alpine loop. They work as independent road-trip ideas, with practical advice for guests planning to drive our Ferrari 458 Italia from Zurich.

1. Lake Zurich, Rapperswil, Zug and Lucerne

Suggested itinerary: Zurich → Meilen → Rapperswil → Pfäffikon SZ → Zug → Lucerne → Zurich. Use the lakeside road on the outward leg and faster connecting roads where useful. Setting each stop in your navigation avoids an itinerary that bypasses the scenery entirely.

This is the most flexible of the three routes. The eastern shore of Lake Zurich gives you water views and small towns before you reach Rapperswil. Park in a designated space and explore the lakefront on foot; the castle viewpoint is a worthwhile option if you have time for a walk. The promenade is a destination for your stop, not a road to drive along.

Continue towards Zug for a waterfront break, then choose whether Lucerne still fits your day. In Lucerne, leave the car in suitable parking and walk to the lake or old town. Trying to include a long lunch, several sightseeing visits and every photographic stop will quickly turn a half-day plan into a full-day outing.

A shorter Lake Zurich drive for a three-hour rental

For a short experience, leave out Zug and Lucerne. Plan a section of Lake Zurich with one stop, or a compact loop through the Zurich Oberland from Nänikon. The exact turnaround point should depend on traffic and your return time. Unlimited kilometres do not make a distant destination fit a short rental.

Season: this route avoids high mountain passes, making it a more flexible alternative in spring and autumn. That does not guarantee suitable Ferrari conditions in winter: ice, snow, visibility and the vehicle's seasonal availability still matter.

Ask us to plan a Lake Zurich drive and tell us whether you prefer an easy lakeside stop or a longer day including Lucerne.

2. Brünig Pass, Lake Brienz and Iseltwald

Suggested itinerary: Zurich → Lucerne → Sarnen → Lungern → Brünig Pass → Brienz → Iseltwald → Interlaken, returning via the Brünig and Lucerne. You can shorten the day by turning back before Interlaken.

This route offers a clear change of scenery. After the approach via Lucerne, the road towards Lungern and the Brünig brings more curves and mountain views. Beyond the pass, Lake Brienz becomes the focus. Brienz suits a waterfront walk; Iseltwald offers another lakeside stop, with village access and parking signs determining where you can leave the car.

Choose one or two substantial stops rather than collecting every viewpoint. An unhurried lunch and a walk by the water usually make a better day than a rushed checklist. Interlaken is an optional extension; it is not necessary to reach it to enjoy Lake Brienz.

Allow enough time and kilometres

We would normally discuss a 12-hour rental for this itinerary. A six-hour booking can leave little flexibility for traffic, lunch and the return journey. A 300 km allowance may be close to the total once pickup and local detours are included, so compare the final route with the available packages before booking.

The Brünig is generally operated year-round, unlike the three high passes below, but temporary restrictions and winter conditions remain possible. Check the current pass information before departure. An open road is not, by itself, confirmation that conditions suit your rental.

3. Susten, Grimsel and Furka: a full Alpine driving day

Suggested itinerary: Zurich → Wassen → Susten Pass → Innertkirchen → Grimsel Pass → Gletsch → Furka Pass → Realp → Andermatt → Wassen → Zurich. This order connects the passes into a loop, rather than treating them as three separate out-and-back trips.

The attraction is the variety: the valley approach to the Susten, the exposed rock and reservoirs around the Grimsel, and the climbing bends towards the Furka. The pass circuit itself is only part of the day. TCS describes a roughly 120 km three-pass circuit from Meiringen; you must also allow for the substantial drive to and from Zurich.

For this version from Zurich, plan a full day and choose a mileage allowance with room for detours. Our 12-hour 500 km or unlimited-kilometre options are more suitable starting points for discussion than a short rental. A longer booking can make sense if you want an overnight stay, subject to availability and agreed parking arrangements.

When should you choose the high passes?

Only when all three roads are open and the forecast is suitable. Opening and closing dates vary with snow and weather; do not assume a calendar month guarantees access. Check Alpen-Pässe when planning and again shortly before departure. If a pass is closed, choose another itinerary rather than assuming navigation will find an equally suitable shortcut.

This is a tiring day for someone unfamiliar with mountain roads. Leave space around cyclists and buses, stop only in permitted places and allow generous time for the return. For a first Ferrari experience, a lake route or one carefully chosen pass may be more enjoyable. Our Swiss Alpine driving guide helps you explore alternatives.

Practical preparation for your Swiss road trip

  • Build in a return buffer. Traffic near Zurich and Lucerne can change a comfortable itinerary. Plan your last sightseeing stop well before the return deadline.
  • Choose suitable parking. Check steep ramps, kerbs and available space before entering with a low sports car. Walk the final stretch to a viewpoint where necessary.
  • Pack lightly. Tell us about luggage before arrival. Small soft bags are easier to accommodate than several rigid suitcases.
  • Prepare before moving. Enter your stops while parked, check fuel and follow signed restrictions. Scenic routes are public roads, not a setting for testing the car's limits.

Frequently asked questions

Which scenic drive near Zurich is best for a first visit?

A Lake Zurich outing is the most adaptable choice in this guide. You can shorten it easily and enjoy a lakeside stop without committing to a long mountain day. Add Zug and Lucerne only if your available time allows.

Can I drive the Susten, Grimsel and Furka passes in one day from Zurich?

Yes, when all passes are open and conditions are suitable, but allow roughly 10–12 hours with stops for this itinerary. It is a full driving day, not a half-day excursion. The exact plan depends on traffic, weather and your experience.

Is Lake Brienz suitable for a six-hour Ferrari rental?

The return journey can take much of the booking. For the route described here, we recommend discussing twelve hours so there is time for breaks and unexpected delays.

Do these routes work in winter?

The high-pass loop is seasonal. Lower lake routes may remain accessible, but Ferrari availability and suitability depend on conditions. We agree an appropriate route with you rather than promising winter access.

Yes. Send your date, pickup point, available hours and whether you prefer lakes or mountains. We can suggest an itinerary and package for your plans. Availability and conditions are confirmed personally.
