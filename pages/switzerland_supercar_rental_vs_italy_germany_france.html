Supercar rental in Europe: Italy, Germany, France or Switzerland?

Lake Como or Portofino, Bodensee or Alsace—or a Swiss escape? Compare the destinations, the collection point and the time you need for a memorable drive.

Published 13 Feb 2026 · Updated 23 Oct 2026

A Ferrari beside Lake Como, a coastal escape to Portofino, a drive around Lake Constance or a weekend among the vineyards of Alsace: each suggests a different European holiday. When comparing supercar rental in Italy, Germany, France and Switzerland, the useful question is where the car fits your journey, not simply which country has the most famous roads.

If your holiday is entirely in Italy, Germany or France, collecting a car locally may be the most convenient choice. If you live in Switzerland, fly into Zurich or are combining several countries, a Ferrari collected near Zurich can make sense. You can enjoy a Swiss driving day or discuss a longer journey abroad, with the route and rental conditions agreed beforehand.

Our starting point: Luxury Obsession’s Ferrari 458 Italia is based in Nänikon, near Zurich. These are destination ideas, not overseas rental branches. Use outside Switzerland requires prior written approval; confirm the countries, drivers, insurance arrangements, mileage, return and any supplements before booking.

Supercar rental in Italy: Lake Como or Portofino?

Italy makes an immediate emotional connection with a Ferrari. But Lake Como and Portofino are very different choices: one is a northern Italian lake destination; the other is on the Ligurian coast. Treating them as interchangeable stops can turn an elegant holiday into a rushed transfer.

Lake Como: a natural choice for a longer escape from Switzerland

If you are considering Ferrari rental for Lake Como, first decide whether you want the car for the journey or mainly for local outings. Collecting near Zurich can work well when the drive south is part of the holiday and you will return to Switzerland. An overnight stay gives the journey more breathing room than trying to combine the return drive with several lakeside villages in one day.

Choose one area of the lake as your base and confirm suitable hotel parking before you arrive. Leave time to enjoy the destination on foot or by boat, too. For a supercar, a well-chosen stretch of road is often more rewarding than attempting to circle the entire lake.

Portofino: make the Riviera the destination

Portofino offers a different mood: a small harbour, colourful waterfront buildings and the Ligurian coastline. We would plan it as a multi-day holiday from Zurich, with time for the journey, the stay and the return. It is not a sensible substitute for a short Swiss driving experience.

For a trip focused entirely on the Italian Riviera, a local rental can be more practical. If you prefer to start and finish in Switzerland, discuss the complete itinerary with us first. Arrange parking and check current vehicle access before approaching the village; the harbour view is somewhere to enjoy at leisure, rather than a promise of driving to every photographed spot.

If Ferrari heritage is the main reason for travelling to Italy, our separate Maranello road-trip article covers that experience in detail.

Supercar rental in Germany: consider Lake Constance

Searching for a sports car rental in Germany does not have to mean planning around the Autobahn. Lake Constance, Bodensee in German, offers a quieter kind of appeal, with lakeside towns such as Konstanz and Meersburg and opportunities to combine driving with a waterfront stop.

For someone starting near Zurich or living in eastern Switzerland, the collection point matters more than the border on a map. A car collected near home may fit the day better than travelling farther to collect one in Germany. Conversely, if your holiday begins and ends in a German city, collecting there may save an unnecessary detour.

Choose one shore or a small number of stops rather than making a complete circuit the goal. There is a car ferry between Konstanz and Meersburg, but any crossing should be checked against the rental agreement and the car’s loading clearance. Include ferry time and costs if you use it. The attraction here is the landscape and the drive, not a high-speed itinerary.

Supercar rental in France: Alsace from a Swiss starting point

For a Swiss-based trip, Alsace is a more natural French destination to consider than Paris or the Côte d’Azur. Around Colmar, Riquewihr and the vineyard villages, the appeal is leisurely touring: changing views, attractive places to stop and time for a good meal.

If you are comparing luxury car rental in France with collection near Zurich, build the decision around the whole journey. A weekend allows you to enjoy the destination without spending most of a single rental day on the outward and return legs. Pick a modest section of the Alsace Wine Route; there is no need to cover the entire route to enjoy the region.

The photograph shows the vineyards around Riquewihr. It is inspiration for the region, not a suggestion to drive along vineyard tracks. Stay on permitted public roads, arrange suitable parking outside tight historic centres where appropriate, and save wine tasting for after the driving is finished. If your stay is entirely in France, compare a local collection option as well.

Switzerland: a complete experience without crossing a border

Sometimes the best answer to a search for a Ferrari holiday abroad is to enjoy the car closer to home. Swiss lakes and mountain scenery can make the driving itself the occasion, particularly when your available time is limited. The 458 Italia’s naturally aspirated V8, responsive steering and sense of occasion do not need a distant destination to feel special.

Lauterbrunnen illustrates the scale of the scenery available in Switzerland. From Zurich, treat the Bernese Oberland as a proper full-day outing or an overnight escape, rather than a quick local loop. Plan where to park and leave time to explore; nearby car-free Wengen is reached onward from the valley, not in the rental car.

Compare the complete rental, not just the daily price

A cheaper advertised day rate does not necessarily produce a cheaper holiday. Equally, Switzerland is not automatically the best-value choice for every itinerary. Ask for a quote that matches your actual plans:

  • Collection and return: include the time and cost of reaching the car and bringing it back.
  • Total distance: count the outward journey, local driving and return, not just the scenic section.
  • Permission and cover: confirm every country and driver, the applicable excess, roadside assistance and any cross-border supplement.
  • Time at the destination: allow for meals, parking, fuel stops and traffic, as well as driving.
  • Practical fit: check luggage space, hotel parking, seasonal conditions and local access requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Can I rent your Ferrari in Switzerland and drive to Italy, Germany or France?

Only with prior written approval. Share your itinerary before booking so the countries, mileage, insurance arrangements, return and any supplements can be confirmed. Overseas travel is not automatically included.

Can I collect the Ferrari at Lake Como, Portofino, Bodensee or in Alsace?

This guide describes trips starting from our base in Nänikon near Zurich, with an agreed return. It does not advertise rental branches or guaranteed delivery at those destinations.

Should I rent locally abroad or collect a Ferrari in Switzerland?

If your entire holiday is abroad, local collection may be simpler. Swiss collection is worth considering when you live here, arrive in Zurich or want the journey from Switzerland to be part of the experience. Compare the complete trip, not only the headline rate.

Which destination would you choose for a shorter rental?

For limited time, we would start with a Swiss route close to Zurich. Lake Constance can suit a more focused day trip with approval. Lake Como, Alsace and especially Portofino become more enjoyable when you allow enough time for the destination and the return.
