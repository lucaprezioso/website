Why drive to Maranello in a Ferrari 458?

There is something unusually satisfying about reaching Maranello in a Ferrari. The badge on the steering wheel suddenly belongs to the place outside the windscreen. After spending time with the car on the road, a museum visit feels connected to an experience you are already living.

The 458 Italia makes that connection particularly vivid. Its naturally aspirated 4.5 litre V8, low driving position and immediate responses turn even ordinary stretches into part of the occasion. A longer rental gives you time to appreciate the quieter side too: an unhurried departure, a scenic stop and an evening with the car parked outside. Our Ferrari 458 vs 488 comparison explores the character that makes this model so appealing.

Davos, Flüela and Fuorn: the scenic road to Merano

The clients approached Italy through Davos and Val Müstair, making the mountains part of the experience rather than simply a crossing to get out of the way. For a route like this, the Flüela Pass and the Fuorn Pass, also known as the Ofenpass, give the journey two distinct Alpine moods.

Flüela Pass: open mountain scenery

From Davos, the Flüela route climbs into a rocky, open landscape before descending towards the Engadine. It is a road to enjoy with time in hand: the changing views and the Ferrari’s precise responses are more memorable when there is no rush to reach the next stop.

Fuorn / Ofen Pass: forests and the approach to Val Müstair

From Zernez, the route towards the Fuorn Pass changes the scenery again. Forested stretches give way to the valley approach, with Val Müstair opening the way towards South Tyrol and Merano. It is a rewarding basis for this journey without adding the Stelvio’s tight sequence of hairpins.

A night in Merano before Maranello

The clients spent a night in Merano before continuing to Maranello. That pause makes sense: after a full Alpine day, an evening walk and a good night’s sleep let the next stage begin at a different pace. For a trip entirely within Switzerland, our Ferrari journey around Andermatt offers another way to combine mountain roads and overnight stays.

The Stelvio Pass: an optional detour, not a shortcut

The Stelvio is an optional detour for drivers who particularly want its dramatic hairpins. It was not part of this client journey. For this trip, we favour the approach via Fuorn and Val Müstair when the priority is a more relaxed drive rather than adding another demanding pass.

The Stelvio’s tight bends and exposure leave less room for error, particularly with traffic or poor visibility. No mountain route is automatically safe: check the weather, seasonal opening and road conditions for every pass, including Flüela and Fuorn. If conditions deteriorate, choose a suitable lower-altitude route instead.

The Ferrari Museum and factory visits in Maranello

Maranello is the emotional centre of the journey. At the Ferrari Museum, the road cars and racing story give context to the badge you have been looking at throughout the drive. Leave time to enjoy the visit rather than squeezing it between two long driving days.

When planning a Ferrari factory tour, distinguish between museum admission, any available panoramic bus tour and access inside production facilities. These are different experiences with separate booking and access conditions. Renting a Ferrari does not provide owner privileges or guarantee a factory visit. Confirm the experience available for your dates directly through the official Ferrari Museums website. Museum tickets and tours are arranged separately from the car rental.

Lake Como: time to slow the journey down

After Maranello, the clients stopped in the Lake Como area before returning to Switzerland. The lake brings a welcome contrast to the mountains and the excitement of Ferrari’s hometown: water, a stroll along the shore and time to let the journey settle.

Our suggested agenda includes one night here so the return does not become an extension of an already long day. Choose accommodation with suitable parking, check access for the Ferrari and leave enough mileage for local driving as well as the journey home.

Returning via San Bernardino instead of the Gotthard

On the way home, the clients chose the San Bernardino Pass to avoid traffic on the Gotthard route. From the Lake Como area, a return via Bellinzona and San Bernardino can give the final leg another Alpine character before continuing towards Thusis and Zurich.

This was a choice for their travel day, not a guarantee that San Bernardino is always quicker or quieter. Check live traffic and pass opening before deciding. When following the suggested map, make sure the route uses the pass road if that is your intention: the San Bernardino tunnel is a different option.

Their one change for next time: take a whole week

The clients already had other holiday plans for the weekend, which is why Weekday Privilege suited their schedule. Afterwards, they told us that if they could plan the journey again, they would take a whole week to enjoy it more.

That is perhaps the most useful lesson from the trip. A full week means less pressure to move on: another evening in Maranello, more time around the lake or a spare day to adapt to Alpine weather. Both Weekday Privilege and our Full Week package can be starting points for the rental, with Italy authorisation and the right mileage agreed separately.

Planning your Ferrari rental for Italy

Can I drive your rental Ferrari from Switzerland to Italy?

Italy travel is possible by prior written agreement and with the applicable supplement. It must be confirmed before departure; it is not automatically included in a standard rental.

Can I collect the Ferrari in Maranello?

The experience described here starts with collection in Nänikon, near Zurich, and an agreed return near Zurich. It is designed for a road trip from Switzerland to Italy.

Can I add kilometres to Weekday Privilege?

Yes, a larger mileage allowance can be agreed for your trip. Ask for a personalised offer covering the full journey, including local drives and the return to Switzerland.

Does the rental include a Ferrari factory tour or museum entry?

No. Museum tickets and tours are booked separately. Renting the car does not guarantee factory access or confer Ferrari owner privileges.

Is the Stelvio Pass part of the suggested main route?

No. The main suggestion runs via Flüela, Fuorn and Val Müstair towards Merano. The Stelvio is an optional, more demanding detour to consider only when conditions and your confidence suit it.

Would a whole week be better for this Ferrari trip?

The clients chose Weekday Privilege because they had other holiday plans for the weekend. Afterwards they said they would choose a full week if planning it again, to enjoy the journey at a more relaxed pace.
